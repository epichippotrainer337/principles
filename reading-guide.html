Weeks 1–2 · Engineering, information and systems

The essential ideas

Engineering applies scientific principles to design practical solutions under constraints such as safety, cost, efficiency and reliability. Technology means the tools, techniques and processes used to achieve practical goals. Designing a bridge is engineering; its construction methods and tools are technologies.

TermMeaningExample
DataRaw observations.18, 26, 35
InformationData with meaningful context.Bus waits were 18, 26 and 35 minutes.
KnowledgeUnderstanding applied to interpret or act.Allow more time or improve the schedule.
ContextCircumstances that determine meaning.Which route, units, dates and time of day?

A pattern is not an explanation. The numbers 1,4,9,16 could be squares or daily sales counts. Find out what was measured.

A system has related parts working toward a goal: input → processing → output. Feedback carries information about the output back to influence future operation. A control system uses feedback to regulate performance. In a thermostat, measured temperature is compared with a target and the heater is adjusted.

Cybernetics, associated with Norbert Wiener, studies control, communication and feedback. An information system collects, processes, stores and distributes information to support its goals. A computer-based IS has hardware, software, databases, telecommunications, people and procedures. Do not omit people and procedures.

Statistics: supporting explanation

The outline includes statistics. It helps summarize and interpret data. For 2,3,4,5,36, the mean is 10 but the median is 4: the outlier pulls up the mean. A biased sample can mislead even when arithmetic is correct. Correlation does not by itself establish causation.

Recall: map an online shop to a system and its six components.

Orders are inputs; price and stock checks are processing; confirmations are outputs; payment/delivery status provides feedback. Servers are hardware; shop programs are software; order records form a database; networks provide telecommunications; customers/staff are people; ordering/refund rules are procedures.

Recall: how can a cheap solution still be poor engineering?

It may fail safety, reliability, usability or other constraints. Engineering balances multiple requirements, not price alone.

Week 3 · Binary and Boolean logic

Analog varies continuously; digital uses discrete representations. Binary circuits interpret signal ranges as 0 or 1, making small noise less likely to change the logical value. A bit has two values; a byte has 8 bits. With n bits there are 2ⁿ patterns.

Unsigned range: 0…2ⁿ−1. Three RGB channels with 8 bits each allow 2²⁴ = 16,777,216 colors. Course units are decimal: KB=10³ bytes, MB=10⁶, GB=10⁹, followed by TB, PB, EB, ZB, YB in factors of 1000. KiB=1024 bytes is a distinct binary unit. b means bits; B means bytes.

Conversions

Worked: 45 = 32+8+4+1 → 101101₂. Pad and group four bits from the right: 0010 1101 → 2D₁₆. Check: 2×16+13=45. One hex digit corresponds to four bits.

Decimal→binary also works by repeated division by 2: record remainders and read them backward. Binary addition rules: 0+0=0; 0+1=1; 1+1=10; 1+1+1=11.

Two’s complement

For n-bit signed two’s complement, range is −2ⁿ⁻¹…2ⁿ⁻¹−1. Fix the width first. To negate: flip all bits, add 1 and keep that width. To interpret a negative bit pattern: subtract 2ⁿ from its unsigned value.

3-bit pattern000001010011100101110111
Unsigned01234567
Signed0123−4−3−2−1
3 − 7 using 4 bits:
  3 = 0011
  7 = 0111
 −7 = 1000 + 1 = 1001
0011 + 1001 = 1100
1100 unsigned is 12; signed is 12−16 = −4.

Use four bits to represent +7 as a signed positive number. Three-bit two’s complement cannot represent +7. Sign-magnitude and one’s complement have two zero representations; two’s complement has one and makes subtraction usable with an adder.

Carry is not signed overflow. In four bits, 7+3 produces 1010, but +10 is outside −8…7: signed overflow. In −1+1, discard the carry and obtain 0000 correctly. Adding same-sign operands and getting the opposite sign indicates signed overflow. The most negative value cannot be negated into a positive value at the same width.

Logic and transistors

Transistors work as controlled switches; circuits of switches implement gates. George Boole is associated with Boolean algebra. Moore’s Law describes a historical trend in transistor counts, not a physical law; heat, leakage and fabrication costs constrain scaling.

NOT flips one input. XOR means different inputs. XOR = (A AND NOT B) OR (NOT A AND B). A half-adder uses XOR for sum and AND for carry. NAND and NOR negate AND and OR.

Week 4 · Hardware

The motherboard connects physical components through buses/controllers. Von Neumann architecture has input, memory, ALU, control unit and output. Its key stored-program principle: data and instructions share memory, so a new task can be loaded rather than physically rewired.

PartJob
CUCoordinates execution and control signals.
ALUArithmetic, logic and comparisons.
RegistersTiny, very fast CPU working storage.
PC / IR / ACCNext instruction address / current instruction / intermediate result.

Machine cycle: fetch → decode → execute → store. Clock frequency counts cycles, not instructions: 3 GHz is three billion cycles/second. Word size is the native data width. Multiple cores enable parallel work, but speedup depends on the workload. ISA defines the instructions software can use; microarchitecture is how a chip implements them.

CPU: general control, branching, complex tasks. GPU: many similar calculations in parallel, useful for graphics, matrices and scientific/AI workloads. A GPU does not replace the CPU for every job.

Memory/storageVolatile?Remember
RegistersYesFastest tiny CPU storage.
Cache / SRAMYesNo periodic refresh; fast, expensive per bit. L1 typically fastest/smallest, then L2/L3.
Main RAM / DRAMYesCapacitors need refresh; dense and relatively cheap.
ROM / firmware flashNoBIOS/UEFI startup firmware; modern flash can be updated.
SSDNoFlash, no moving parts, fast random access.
HDDNoMagnetic disks and moving heads; mechanical delay.
Optical / tapeNoDistribution/archives; tape is sequential.

Hierarchy: registers → cache → RAM → SSD → HDD → archival media. Moving downward generally trades speed for capacity and lower cost per bit. “Static” RAM still needs power. ROM is firmware storage, not ordinary working RAM.

Explain opening a calculator and adding 2+3.

The program resides on storage. Needed instructions/data load into RAM. The CPU fetches and decodes instructions; registers hold operands; ALU calculates; results go to memory/display. A saved file persists on storage; unsaved volatile data is lost without power.

Why is clock speed alone a poor performance comparison?

Instructions per cycle, architecture, cache, memory delays, core count and parallelism also matter. Higher frequency does not necessarily mean faster completion.

Weeks 5–6 · Algorithms

An algorithm specifies precise steps to solve a problem. Define inputs, outputs and how it stops. A program implements an algorithm. Its five course actions are name binding, selection, repetition, modularization and recursion.

Name binding: associate an identifier with a value/object/location. Assignment is not mathematical equality: x=x+1 calculates using old x then stores new x. Selection: choose by a condition. Repetition: loop. Modularization: split responsibilities into reusable parts. Recursion: solve smaller instances by calling the same function.

Trace, don’t guess

total = 0
for i from 1 through 4:
    if i % 2 == 0:
        total = total + i
print(total)

After i=1,2,3,4, total is 0,2,2,6. Output: 6. % is remainder. Check whether the upper bound is included and whether each loop makes progress toward termination.

Complexity measures resource growth with input size: time counts growing work, space counts memory. Big O is an asymptotic upper bound, ignoring constants and lower-order terms. Supporting examples: one pass O(n), all pairs O(n²), repeated halving O(log n). Best/average/worst cases concern input situations, not computer brands.

Recursion: base case + progress

factorial(n):                 # non-negative integer
    if n == 0: return 1
    return n * factorial(n-1)

factorial(4) = 4*3*2*1*1 = 24

The base case is the stopping condition. Calls retain unfinished work on the stack; returning unwinds it. Missing a base case or moving away from it can prevent termination. A recursive sum can use sum(0)=0 and sum(n)=n+sum(n−1).

Hanoi: move n−1 disks from source to spare; move largest to destination; move n−1 from spare to destination. Never put a larger disk on a smaller one. Minimum moves 2ⁿ−1: 3 disks need 7. Stop at n=0 or handle n=1 directly.

Dijkstra’s shortest path

Requires non-negative edge weights. Start distance 0, all others infinity. Pick the unvisited node with the smallest tentative distance; finalize it; relax its neighbors. Keep predecessors to reconstruct the path. Stop when the destination is finalized, not when first discovered.

candidate = distance[current] + weight(current, neighbor)
if candidate < distance[neighbor]:
    distance[neighbor] = candidate
    predecessor[neighbor] = current

Worked undirected graph: A–B=4, A–C=1, C–B=2, B–D=1, C–D=5.

Shortest route: A→C→B→D, total 4. Use total distance from the start, not simply the cheapest next edge. A priority queue efficiently selects the smallest distance.

Try: largest of A,B,C including negative inputs and ties.
largest = A
if B > largest: largest = B
if C > largest: largest = C
return largest

Initializing to zero would fail when every input is negative.

Why can negative edges invalidate Dijkstra?

A finalized distance might later improve through a negative edge. Non-negative weights support the finalization guarantee.

Week 7 · Software and operating systems

Programming expresses algorithms in a language. Software includes programs and associated documentation/specifications. Software engineering is systematic development, testing and maintenance.

Syntax is grammatical form; semantics is meaning. “x=5+” is malformed. A correctly written width+height formula is still wrong if intended to calculate rectangular area. Type errors may be detected before or during execution depending on the language.

CompilerInterpreter
Translates to another representation before that translated code executes.Executes operations through a runtime.
Output can be machine code or bytecode.Runtime needed during execution.
Often enables advance optimization; changes may require rebuilding.Often convenient for experiments and incremental execution.

JIT compiles during execution. Real systems combine approaches. Compiled code can still contain logic errors and fail at runtime; “compiled” does not mean correct.

The lecture treats generative AI as a newer interaction/computation paradigm, not a guaranteed-correct next language generation.

Supporting explanations for the learning outcomes

Imperative/procedural: steps and changing state. Object-oriented: objects combine state and behavior. Functional: functions/transformations, often minimizing mutable state. Declarative: specify what is wanted; logic programming uses facts/rules. Languages can support several paradigms.

Good practice: clarify requirements; divide responsibilities; use meaningful names; avoid needless duplication/deep nesting; test normal, boundary and invalid inputs; handle errors; use version control; review code; document decisions; maintain after release.

Software categories and OS

System software: OS, drivers and utilities. Application software: user tasks such as documents, music and image editing. Drivers communicate with specific devices. Utilities perform supporting maintenance tasks.

Proprietary/open-source concerns source rights/licensing. Off-the-shelf/custom-built concerns ready-made versus tailored products. Free-of-charge concerns price. These are independent distinctions: free access does not make Google Workspace open source. LibreOffice is an open-source example.

The OS manages processes, CPU time, memory, files, devices and permissions. Its kernel is the core resource manager; the whole OS also includes libraries, services and interfaces. Ubuntu and Android both use the Linux kernel but are different environments.

When an application saves a file, it requests an OS service; the kernel coordinates access through drivers. Time-sharing allows concurrency on one core; multiple cores also allow simultaneous execution.

Recall: Word, Windows, printer driver, printer—classify them.

Application software; operating system; device driver/system software; hardware.

Recall: why does compilation not guarantee a correct answer?

A valid program may implement the wrong algorithm or encounter runtime conditions not resolved by compilation. Testing is still needed.

Week 5 worksheet · Guided solutions

Open the original 8-slide worksheet. Try each problem first. These are my worked solutions, not an instructor answer key. Rows and columns below are 1-based.

Slide 2 · Eight points on a circle

The figure starts i=1 on the positive x-axis and goes counterclockwise. θ=(i−1)π/4; x=R cos θ, y=R sin θ. At i=3, (x,y)=(0,R); i=2 gives (R/√2,R/√2).

Slide 3 · Five-column snake numbering

For positive N: row=floor((N−1)/5)+1; offset=(N−1)%5. On odd rows column=offset+1; on even rows column=5−offset. Example: N=8 → row 2, offset 2 → column 3.

Slide 4 · Clockwise border of N×N board

For N≥2 there are 4N−4 border cells. Start at (1,1), K=1. Use these four disjoint cases:

K ≤ N:          row=1,          col=K
K ≤ 2N−1:       row=K−N+1,      col=N
K ≤ 3N−2:       row=N,          col=3N−K−1
otherwise:      row=4N−K−2,     col=1

Require 1≤K≤4N−4. Handle N=1 separately: only K=1 at (1,1). For N=8,K=22 → row 8,column 1.

Slide 5 · Triangular rows

Row r ends at triangular number T(r)=r(r+1)/2. Choose the smallest r with T(r)≥N. Formula: r=ceil((√(8N+1)−1)/2). Position=N−r(r−1)/2. For N=8: r=4, position=2. Integer-loop alternative: keep increasing r until its triangular total reaches N.

Slide 6 · Smaller clock angle

Minute angle=6M°. Hour angle=30(H mod 12)+0.5M°. Let d=absolute difference; answer=min(d,360−d). At 3:30, hour=105°, minute=180°, answer=75°. The hour hand moves during the hour!

Slide 7 · Robot spiral

The drawn segments go right, up, left, down, with lengths 1,1,2,2,3,3,4,4… If “movement” means an entire segment, segment j has length ceil(j/2), direction repeating R,U,L,D. Start (0,0); update x/y for each segment. After 4 segments: (−1,−1); after 8: (−2,−2). If movement means one grid step, consume one unit at a time and stop within the current segment. State the interpretation because the slide’s wording is ambiguous.

Slide 8 · Center-out spiral

For odd board size N, center m=(N+1)/2. Let x=C−m, y=R−m (y increases downward), ring k=max(|x|,|y|), maximum M=(2k+1)². For k=0 return 1. Otherwise:

top edge y=−k:    value = M−(k−x)
left edge x=−k:   value = M−2k−(y+k)
bottom edge y=k:  value = M−4k−(x+k)
right edge x=k:   value = M−6k−(k−y)

Check against the figure: top-right=25, top-left=21, bottom-left=17, bottom-right=13 in a 5×5 board. At row 3,column 4 the result is 2.

Rapid recall cards

Cover the answer; say it aloud before opening.

What does the PC hold?

The address of the next instruction.

What is the base case?

The recursive stopping condition.

What are Dijkstra’s weight restrictions?

Non-negative edge weights.

What does XOR output for 1 and 1?

0. OR would output 1.

Does SRAM retain data with power off?

No. Static means no periodic refresh while powered.

What is the range of signed 4-bit two’s complement?

−8 through 7.

Is a free application necessarily open source?

No. Price and source-code rights are different.

What makes feedback useful?

It returns information about results so future inputs/processes can be adjusted.

Which is bigger: one byte or one bit?

One byte = eight bits.

What is an ISA?

The instruction set/interface visible to software; microarchitecture implements it.

Clarifications to keep you from learning a typo

  • The binary note reverses decimal/binary powers in one sentence: decimal uses powers of 10; binary powers of 2.
  • A byte can encode an ASCII character, but modern Unicode characters may use multiple bytes.
  • Carry-out and signed overflow are different. Always state width and interpretation.
  • One’s complement wastes one extra zero representation, not “three bits.”
  • Off-the-shelf, proprietary and open-source are not opposite ends of one classification.
  • Higher clock speed or more cores alone does not guarantee faster execution.
